# Pitch deck

A pitch deck makes a case in 10–15 slides. Each slide answers one question the audience is asking themselves; the order follows their scepticism.

## Decide first
- Who decides and what they need to believe to say yes.
- The ask: amount, partnership, contract, vote.
- Read alone or presented? Read-alone decks carry a bit more text; presented decks less.

## The classic sequence (adapt, do not pad)
1. **Title**: name + one-line what you do.
2. **Problem**: who has it, how painful, a number or a story.
3. **Solution**: what you do, shown (screenshot, photo, demo GIF frame).
4. **Why now**.
5. **Market**: who buys, how many, how you reach them (bottom-up, not "1% of a trillion").
6. **Product / how it works**.
7. **Traction**: users, revenue, pilots — a chart that goes up, honestly.
8. **Business model**: who pays what.
9. **Competition**: 2×2 positioning or a comparison table.
10. **Team**: faces, names, why these people.
11. **Finances / plan**: milestones and use of funds.
12. **The ask** and contact.

- **One number per slide** when the number is the argument.
- **Charts drawn in SVG** with the claim as the title ("Revenue tripled in 6 months").
- **Real visuals**: product screenshots, photos of the team; no stock people.

## Make it distinctive
Carry the product's identity: its colour as the accent, its tone in the copy. A logistics startup can use a route-line motif; a food brand, real food photography. Keep the system consistent across all slides.

## Avoid
- Buzzwords, made-up market sizes, unlabelled charts.
- Team slides with only titles and no reason to trust them.
- More than 15 slides for a first pitch.

## Self-check
1. Can a stranger state the problem and solution after slide 3?
2. Are numbers sourced and consistent?
3. Is the ask explicit and specific?
4. Is each slide one question answered?
5. Text large enough to read on a shared screen?
