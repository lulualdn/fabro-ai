# Cover letter

A cover letter answers one question: why you, for this place, now. Its design should match the CV so the two look like a set.

## Decide first
- The organisation and the role, by name. Find one specific thing about them to mention.
- Your three best arguments, each with a proof (a project, a number, an experience).
- Language conventions (French: "Objet : Candidature au poste de…", "Madame, Monsieur,", formal closing).

## Build it
1. Reuse the CV's header (same name style, same accent, same contact line). If there is no CV, design a small letterhead.
2. Recipient, place and date, subject line naming the role and reference.
3. **Paragraph 1 — them**: why this organisation, with a specific detail.
4. **Paragraph 2 — you**: two arguments with proofs, tied to what the job needs.
5. **Paragraph 3 — together**: what you would bring or learn, availability.
6. **Close**: ask for an interview, polite formula, signature.

- Keep it under one page: 250–350 words of body text.
- Bold nothing in the body, or at most the role name once.
- Mirror two or three words from the job advert so the reader recognises the match.

## Make it distinctive — choose one
- **Matching set**: identical header to the CV, same accent rule.
- **Editorial**: a large serif greeting and generous margins, for creative roles.
- **Compact business**: a thin left band in the accent colour holding the sender details.

## Avoid
- Repeating the CV line by line.
- "I am writing to apply for…" as the whole first paragraph.
- Clichés ("dynamic, motivated, team player") without proof.
- Generic text that would fit any company.

## Self-check
1. Is the company's name and one specific detail in paragraph 1?
2. Does every claim about you have a proof?
3. Does it fit one page with comfortable margins?
4. Does it look like a set with the CV?
5. Is the closing formula right for the language?
