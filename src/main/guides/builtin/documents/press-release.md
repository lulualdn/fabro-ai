# Press release

A journalist decides in ten seconds whether there is a story. The format is conventional on purpose: follow it, and make the news itself sharp.

## Decide first
- The news in one sentence: who does what, when, where, why it matters. If you cannot write it, there is no release yet.
- Embargo or "for immediate release".
- One quote worth printing, from a named person.

## Build it
1. **Header**: organisation name or logo, "Communiqué de presse" / "Press release", place and date.
2. **Headline**: the news as a factual sentence, 8–14 words, no hype.
3. **Subhead**: one or two lines adding the key detail or figure.
4. **Lead paragraph**: who, what, when, where, why — complete on its own.
5. **Body**: details, context, numbers, a quote from a named person with role.
6. **Practical information** box: dates, address, prices, links.
7. **About** paragraph (boilerplate) and **press contact** (name, phone, email).
8. End mark: "###" or "Fin".

- One page if possible, two at most.
- Numbers concrete and sourced; dates with the day of the week.
- Quote sounds like a person, not a slogan.

## Make it fit
Sober by convention: the organisation's colour in the header rule and the label only; a photo credit line if images are available for press.

## Avoid
- Superlatives ("revolutionary", "unique", "leader").
- A headline that is a slogan.
- Missing contact details or date.

## Self-check
1. Does the headline state the news factually?
2. Does the first paragraph answer who/what/when/where/why?
3. Is the quote attributed with name and role?
4. Press contact complete?
5. Fits on one or two pages?
