# Business plan

A business plan convinces a banker, investor or jury that the project is realistic. Credibility comes from specific numbers and honest risks, presented clearly.

## Decide first
- The reader and what they check: a bank looks at cash flow and guarantees; an investor at market size and growth; a jury at impact.
- The ask: amount, use of funds, timeline.
- Length: 10–20 pages plus annexes; a two-page executive summary first.

## Build it
1. **Cover**: project name, one-line description, founder(s), date, contact.
2. **Executive summary** (1–2 pages): problem, solution, market, model, team, numbers, ask.
3. **Problem and solution**.
4. **Market**: size, target customers, competitors (comparison table), positioning.
5. **Offer and pricing**.
6. **Marketing and sales plan**.
7. **Operations**: suppliers, premises, legal status.
8. **Team**.
9. **Finances**: start-up costs, 3-year forecast (revenue, costs, result), cash plan, break-even — tables plus one or two charts.
10. **Risks and mitigations**.
11. **Annexes**.

## Techniques
- **Financial tables** right-aligned, tabular figures, totals ruled, negative numbers in parentheses or a muted red (technique/tables).
- **Break-even chart** in SVG: revenue and cost lines crossing, the crossing labelled with the month.
- **Competitor matrix**: a 2×2 positioning diagram (price × quality, etc.) with your project in the accent.
- **Key numbers** repeated in the summary must match the finance section exactly.

## Make it distinctive
The project's world sets the tone: a food truck plan in warm colours with a photo of the truck; a SaaS in clean blue-grey with product screenshots; an association with portraits of the people it helps. Keep data pages calm.

## Avoid
- Optimistic numbers without assumptions (state them: price × customers × months).
- Market sizes for the whole world when you serve one town.
- Hiding risks.

## Self-check
1. Is the ask clear on page 2?
2. Do all numbers add up and match across sections?
3. Are assumptions stated?
4. Competitors named honestly?
5. Risks listed with responses?
